Summary of Abstract and Conclusion
Article title: Leveraging Feature Selection and Ensemble Learning to Predict Secondary School Achievement: A Comparative Study of Three Grade Granularities
1. Summary of the Abstract
The article focuses on using machine learning to predict students' academic performance. The main purpose of the research is to identify students who may have difficulties at school and help educators take action earlier.
The researchers used a dataset of 395 students from two secondary schools in Portugal. They studied three types of academic outcomes: low, medium, and high performance; four grade categories (fail, satisfactory, good, and excellent); and pass or fail results.
The study compared nine machine learning algorithms and three feature selection methods using the WEKA software. The researchers applied ten-fold cross-validation to evaluate the models.
The results showed that Random Forest with Information Gain achieved 90.7% accuracy in predicting three performance levels. Bagging with Information Gain achieved 92.5% accuracy in predicting pass or fail results.
The most important factors were students' previous grades and their history of academic failures. The study shows that combining feature selection and ensemble learning can improve prediction accuracy.
2. Summary of the Conclusion
The conclusion shows that ensemble learning methods, especially Random Forest and Bagging, achieved the best results in predicting students' academic performance.
The research confirmed that previous academic grades (G1 and G2) and the number of past failures are the strongest predictors of future academic results. Other factors, such as parents' education and occupation, family background, and additional educational support, also influence prediction accuracy.
The researchers also found that models became less accurate when previous grades were excluded. However, other demographic, behavioral, and family-related factors still helped identify students who might be at risk of academic failure.
An important finding is that machine learning can help teachers identify students whose problems are not immediately visible through classroom observation. For example, absenteeism, low motivation, and limited family support may indicate academic risks.
The authors recommend using machine learning as a supporting tool for teachers, not as a replacement for them. They also explain that the study has limitations because the dataset comes from only two Portuguese schools. Future research should use data from other countries, schools, and academic years to test whether the results can be applied in different educational systems.
3. Overall Conclusion
Overall, the study demonstrates that machine learning and feature selection can help predict students' academic performance with high accuracy. Previous academic results are the most important predictors, but social and family factors also provide useful information. These technologies can support teachers in identifying students at risk and providing timely educational support.
